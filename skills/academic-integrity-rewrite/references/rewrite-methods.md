# Rewrite methods

Use the lightest transformation that produces clearer, genuinely authored prose.

## By overlap type

### Phrase overlap

- Remove formulaic openings and redundant transitions.
- Change the grammatical center only when meaning remains exact.
- Combine or divide sentences based on information structure.
- Retain technical names, legal phrases, and properly quoted text when precision requires them.

### Structural overlap

- Rebuild paragraph order from the evidence map.
- Lead with the research question, contrast, or result rather than mirroring the source.
- Integrate related evidence into one synthesis and move limitations next to the claims they constrain.

### Idea overlap

- Attribute the source. Paraphrasing never removes the need to cite.
- Add the author's comparison, interpretation, boundary condition, or research gap when supported.
- Quote distinctive language when its exact wording matters.

## By manuscript section

### Abstract

Use one compact chain: context → unresolved problem → method → quantitative findings → contribution. Preserve all numbers and avoid unsupported novelty claims.

### Introduction and literature review

Organize sources by theme, method, agreement, conflict, or gap. Avoid one paragraph per source and citation dumping. Make clear which assessment is the current author's synthesis.

### Methods

Preserve reproducibility. Keep standard terminology, parameters, equations, software versions, and boundary conditions exact. Rewrite surrounding explanation and cite established procedures. Do not obscure copied protocol text that needs quotation, permission, or attribution.

### Results

Lead with the comparison and quantitative result. Separate observation from explanation. Preserve signs, baselines, uncertainty, units, and figure/table references.

### Discussion

Use claim → evidence → comparison with prior work → plausible mechanism → limitation. Qualify mechanisms that the experiment or model does not directly resolve.

### Conclusion

Synthesize answers to the research question rather than repeating the abstract. Retain decisive quantities and boundaries; avoid introducing new evidence.

## Prohibited shortcuts

- Do not translate out and back to hide copied text.
- Do not mechanically swap synonyms or alter word order.
- Do not delete citations after paraphrasing.
- Do not change values, units, labels, sample sizes, or statistical meaning to reduce matches.
- Do not invent sources, DOIs, experiments, limitations, or author contributions.
- Do not insert invisible characters or manipulate document formatting to fool a detector.
